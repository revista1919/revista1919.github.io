01. Important Notice on Editorial Policies

Submitting a manuscript to the National Review of Sciences for Students implies full and unreserved acceptance of the entirety of our Editorial Policies. We urge you to read them carefully before preparing your contribution. Failure to comply with any of their provisions —regarding originality, ethics, authorship, conflicts of interest, or any other matter— may result in the rejection of the submission at any stage of the editorial process.

See also: Editorial Policies · Open Access Policies · Peer Review Policy · Good Practices Guide

02. Accepted Manuscript Types

The Journal accepts contributions in Spanish and English. Before preparing your manuscript, identify the article type that corresponds to your work. Each type has specific structure, content, and length requirements.

3,000 – 8,000 words

Original Research Article

Presents unpublished results of empirical or theoretical scientific research, structured under the IMRaD format (Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion) or its disciplinary equivalent. Contributions from the natural and exact sciences as well as the humanities and social sciences are accepted.

Structure: Introduction → Methods (Design, Participants, Instruments, Procedure, Analysis) → Results → Discussion → Conclusion → References

3,000 – 6,000 words

Academic Essay

Argumentative text that develops an original thesis on a scientific, philosophical, or humanistic topic, supported by a critical review of existing literature. IMRaD structure is not required, but a rigorous critical apparatus is essential.

Structure: Clear thesis → Evidence-based arguments → Counterarguments → Conclusion

1,500 – 3,000 words

Reflective Essay

Exposition of personal reflections on an academic experience, reading, or practice, with well-founded theoretical connections.

Structure: Experience → Personal analysis → Theoretical connection → Learning

2,000 – 4,000 words

Case Report

Detailed presentation of one or more particular cases —clinical, experimental, field study, educational intervention— whose interest lies in their uniqueness, formative value, or potential to generate hypotheses.

Structure: Context → Detailed description → Analysis → Lessons learned

4,000 – 10,000 words

Systematic Review

Manuscript that critically synthesizes available evidence on a specific research question, following an explicit, reproducible, and documented methodology of search, selection, quality assessment, and synthesis.

Structure: PICO question → Search strategy → Flow diagram → Synthesis → Conclusions

1,000 – 2,000 words

Book Review

Critical analysis of a recently published academic or scientific dissemination work of demonstrable relevance to the field of study.

Structure: Bibliographic data → Objective summary → Critical analysis → Recommendation

Unsure about the type? Write to editor@revistacienciasestudiantes.com with a 100-word summary of your idea and the editorial team will guide you.

03. Document Format and Word Processors

3.1. Mandatory Format

All manuscripts must be submitted exclusively in Microsoft Word (.docx) format. This is the standard working format for the Journal's review, editing, and typesetting process. Submissions in other formats (PDF, LaTeX, RTF, TXT, Google Docs, etc.) will not be accepted.

3.2. Instructions for LaTeX Users

The Journal recognizes that LaTeX is widely used in disciplines such as mathematics, physics, and engineering. However, to standardize the editorial workflow, the final manuscript must be delivered in .docx format. The use of Pandoc for conversion is recommended:

pandoc archivo.tex --citeproc --csl=chicago-author-date.csl -o resultado.docx

It is the author's sole responsibility to verify that the conversion has faithfully preserved the content, mathematical formulas, cross-references, and bibliographic citations.

3.3. Font Format and Styles

Font type, size, line spacing, and margins are not relevant for the initial evaluation, as the manuscript will be automatically standardized during the typesetting process. The essential point is that the content is clear and that the structure is hierarchized through the correct use of headings (see Section 05).

Optional formatting suggestions: Times New Roman, size 12, 1.5 line spacing, 2.5 cm margins. These are visual references only, not mandatory requirements.

04. Anonymization for Double-Blind Review

4.1. General Principle

The Journal operates a strict double-blind peer review system. The reviewer does not know the author's identity and vice versa. To ensure its integrity, the submitted manuscript must not contain any information that could identify its authors.

4.2. Elements That Must Be Removed or Anonymized

  • Author names and institutional affiliations on the title page and anywhere else in the manuscript.
  • Email addresses, phone numbers, and postal addresses of the authors.
  • Acknowledgments that mention individuals, institutions, or identifying projects. Removed for review and restored in the final accepted version.
  • Funding information with grant codes or references to specific projects. Use generic descriptions if strictly necessary.
  • Self-citations that reveal identity. Use "(Author, year)" in the text and "[Anonymized for review]" in the reference list.
  • .docx file metadata: Use Word's "Inspect Document" tool to remove personal data.
  • Names of unique institutions or laboratories in Methods. Use generic descriptions (e.g., "a university hospital in the metropolitan region").
The two-file rule:
File A (for submission): Completely anonymous
File B (for your records): With all complete data
Upon acceptance, you will submit File B for final publication.

05. Manuscript Structure and Use of Headings

5.1. The Importance of Headings

It is mandatory to use the heading functions (Heading 1, Heading 2, Heading 3, etc.) of your word processor. Do not simulate structure with bold text or larger font sizes. This is vital because:

  • Automatic Styling: The Journal applies automatic styles to manuscripts with real headings.
  • Navigation: Generates a navigation panel for editors and reviewers.
  • Accessibility: Essential for assistive technologies (screen readers).

5.2. Correct Heading Hierarchy

Level Word Style LaTeX Equivalent Recommended Use
1 Heading 1 \section{} Main sections: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, References
2 Heading 2 \subsection{} Subsections: Participants, Instruments, Procedure
3 Heading 3 \subsubsection{} Specific subdivisions: Demographic Questionnaire, Well-Being Scale

Warning: A manuscript that presents its sections only with bold text and without using the word processor's heading styles may be returned for correction before the review process begins.

5.3. Recommended IMRaD Structure

Example of correct hierarchy:
Heading 1: Introduction
Heading 1: Methods
  Heading 2: Design
  Heading 2: Participants
  Heading 2: Instruments
  Heading 2: Procedure
  Heading 2: Data Analysis
Heading 1: Results
Heading 1: Discussion
Heading 1: Conclusion
Heading 1: References

06. The Abstract – The Gateway

6.1. Requirements

  • Length: Between 150 and 250 words. Excludes bibliographic citations and complex formulas.
  • Language: Required in Spanish and English (Abstract) with faithful technical translation.
  • Content: Context → Objective → Methods → Results → Conclusion
Structural Example (Original Research):
"This study examines the effect of classical music on the concentration of university students. Using an experimental design with 60 participants, performance on an attention test was compared with and without background music. Results show a 15% improvement in short-term memory tasks with instrumental music (p < 0.05), although without differences in reasoning tasks. It is concluded that ambient music is a viable tool to optimize specific cognitive processes in academic settings."
Book Review Example:
"This review analyzes Richard Dawkins' 'The Selfish Gene,' evaluating its contribution to scientific dissemination and its relevance after four decades. The central concepts proposed by the author are examined, contrasting them with later developments in evolutionary biology. The work stands out for its expository clarity, although it presents limitations in the discussion of altruism and group selection. It is recommended as essential reading for biology students and the general public interested in evolution."

07. Recommended Length

7.1. Standard Range

The recommended length for all manuscript types is 1,000 to 10,000 words, including the body of the text, footnotes, acknowledgments (in the final version), and the reference list.

7.2. Guidance by Article Type

Type Length
Book Review1,000 – 2,000 words
Reflective Essay1,500 – 3,000 words
Case Report2,000 – 4,000 words
Academic Essay3,000 – 6,000 words
Original Research3,000 – 8,000 words
Systematic Review4,000 – 10,000 words

7.3. Manuscripts Exceeding the Recommended Length

Manuscripts of greater length will be accepted. However, they will be subject to additional review by the editor:

  • The editor will assess whether the length is fully justified.
  • If deemed excessive, they may suggest a cut as a condition for continuing.
  • In extreme cases (more than 20,000 words), they may suggest division into two or more publications.

08. Citation Style: Chicago 17th ed. (Author-Date)

8.1. General Rule

All manuscripts, without exception, must format their scholarly apparatus according to the Chicago Manual of Style, 17th edition, in its author-date variant. Correct and consistent use of this style is a requirement for acceptance.

8.2. In-Text Citations

Examples:
Paraphrase: Evolution is a gradual process (Darwin 1859)
Direct quote: "Natural selection acts slowly" (Darwin 1859, 78)
Two authors: (Pérez and González 2022)
Three or more authors: (Smith et al. 2021)

8.3. Reference List

Book:
Binder, Amy J. 2022. The Channels of Student Activism: How the Left and Right Are Winning (and Losing) in Campus Politics Today. Chicago: University of Chicago Press.

Book chapter:
Doyle, Kathleen. 2023. "The Queen Mary Psalter." In The Book by Design: The Remarkable Story of the World's Greatest Invention, edited by P. J. M. Marks and Stephen Parkin, 60-73. Chicago: University of Chicago Press.

Journal article:
Dittmar, Emily L., and Douglas W. Schemske. 2023. "Temporal Variation in Selection Influences Microgeographic Local Adaptation." American Naturalist 202 (4): 471-85. https://doi.org/10.1086/725865.

Web page:
Google. 2023. "Privacy Policy." Privacy & Terms. Effective November 15. https://policies.google.com/privacy.
Recommendation: Use free bibliographic managers such as Zotero or Mendeley. They automatically export in Chicago 17 format.

09. Keywords and Controlled Vocabularies

9.1. Keywords

  • Quantity: Minimum 2, maximum 6
  • Language: Spanish and English
  • Criteria: Specific terms, not general ones. Combine controlled vocabulary with common terms in your field.

9.2. Specialized Controlled Vocabulary Codes

Additionally, a minimum of two (2) and a maximum of four (4) codes from the controlled vocabulary corresponding to the subject area is required.

Input format: The author must verify the exact code format on the official page of each vocabulary, as not all systems use alphanumeric codes. Some employ only descriptors or terms.

Examples by vocabulary:
JEL (Economics): Uses alphanumeric codes. Example: B14, B51, D46, P16
MeSH (Medicine): Uses alphanumeric codes. Example: D009369
ACM CCS (Computer Science): Uses hierarchical codes. Example: CCS2012.10010179
UNESCO (Humanities): Does not use codes. Enter the exact descriptor or term from the thesaurus. Example: Environmental education

Consult the complete table of controlled vocabularies in the Editorial Policies to find the corresponding thesaurus for your discipline and verify the specific input format.

10. Figures, Tables, and Graphic Material

10.1. Technical Requirements

  • Resolution: Minimum 300 dpi for photographs, 600 dpi or vector for graphics
  • Accepted formats: PNG, TIFF, JPG/JPEG, PDF, SVG
  • Placement: At the approximate location where they are mentioned
  • Captions: Corresponding numbering and description

10.2. Scientific Integrity of Images

Only global, linear, and uniform adjustments of brightness, contrast, color balance, cropping, or sharpness are permitted. Constitutes falsification any manipulation involving adding, removing, moving, or cloning elements of an image that constitutes scientific evidence.

10.3. Third-Party Copyright

Authors are responsible for obtaining the necessary permissions or licenses to reproduce figures, images, or graphic material of which they do not hold the rights.

11. Ethics and Originality

11.1. Unpublished Status and Similarity

Every manuscript must constitute an original work. Textual overlap with previous versions must not exceed 15%. The Journal uses similarity detection tools (currently PlagiarismGuard).

Similarity Percentage Editorial Action
Under 15%Normal start of the review process
15% – 25%Editorial Committee review
25% – 40%Detailed review; possible justification required
Over 40%Return or editorial rejection (desk reject)

11.2. Simultaneous Submissions

Strictly prohibited to submit the same manuscript to another journal while under evaluation. If detected, the manuscript will be immediately withdrawn.

11.3. Authorship and CRediT

Authorship requires the simultaneous concurrence of the four ICMJE criteria: substantial contribution, drafting or critical review, final approval, and responsibility for the work. In the submission form, authors must specify their individual contribution using the CRediT taxonomy.

11.4. Use of Artificial Intelligence

12. Mandatory Declarations

12.1. Data Availability

All manuscripts must include a Data Availability Statement. Deposit in public repositories such as Zenodo, Figshare, OSF, or Dryad is recommended.

Example: "The data supporting this study are available in the institutional repository [URL] or upon reasonable request to the corresponding author."

12.2. Conflicts of Interest and Funding

Example without conflict: "The authors declare no conflicts of interest."

Example with funding: "This study was funded by [agency name], grant [code]."

12.3. Ethical Approval

When the study involves direct interaction with human subjects, interventions, or identifiable personal data, approval from a research ethics committee (IRB) is required. The manuscript must indicate: committee name, approval number, and date.

12.4. AI Usage Declaration

If AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, etc.) were used, you must declare it specifically indicating: tool name and version, specific purpose, and sections where it was employed.

13. Open Data, Code, and Reproducibility

13.1. Data Deposit

Deposit of datasets in recognized public repositories is recommended. Data must be deposited no later than the article's publication date.

13.2. Code Sharing

Strongly recommended to deposit computational code developed or used. Suggested repositories: GitHub (preferably archived via Zenodo to obtain a DOI), GitLab, or OSF. Use of standard open licenses (MIT, BSD 3-Clause, Apache 2.0, GNU GPL v3.0) is recommended.

13.3. Exceptions

The "data available upon reasonable request" modality will be accepted only exceptionally, with express justification (legal, ethical, contractual, or intellectual property restrictions).

15. Editorial Review Flow

1

Technical Screening (Desk Review)

The management team verifies anonymization, format, similarity (plagiarism), and thematic focus.

2

Assignment and Peer Review

A Section Editor assigns the manuscript to 2 or 3 expert reviewers (double-blind).

3

Editorial Decision

  • Accept: Moves directly to production
  • Minor/Major Revisions: Correction and resubmission
  • Reject: Weaknesses are notified
4

Production and Open Publication

Typesetting, DOI assignment, and publication under CC BY 4.0 license (Diamond Open Access).
