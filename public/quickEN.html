1. Manuscript Structure and Format

1.0 The Abstract – Your Gateway

What is it? A brief, self-contained synthesis of your article. It is the main element (and sometimes the only one) that an editor or reviewer reads to decide whether to evaluate your work.

  • Length: Between 150 and 250 words. Excludes bibliographic citations and complex formulas.
  • Suggested IMRaD structure:
    • Context: What is studied and why does it matter? (1–2 sentences)
    • Objective: What were you trying to answer? (1 sentence)
    • Methods: Design, participants, instruments. (1–2 sentences)
    • Results: Key statistical or analytical findings. (2–3 sentences)
    • Conclusion: Implication of your finding. (1 sentence)
  • Language: A version in Spanish and English (Abstract) with faithful technical translation is required.
Structural Example: "This study examines the effect of classical music on the concentration of university students. Using an experimental design with 60 participants, performance on an attention test with and without background music was compared. Results show a 15% improvement in short-term memory tasks with instrumental music (p < 0.05), although there were no differences in reasoning tasks. It is concluded that ambient music is a viable tool to optimize specific cognitive processes in academic settings."
1.1 The File and Double-Blind Preparation
  • Format: Exclusively Microsoft Word (.docx). LaTeX users must convert using Pandoc.
  • Double-Blind Peer Review: To ensure impartiality, reviewers will not know your identity, nor will you know theirs.
  • Anonymization Instructions:
    • Remove author names and affiliations below the title.
    • In the Acknowledgments section, omit names of tutors, scholarships, or universities.
    • If you self-cite, replace your name in the text and bibliography with "[Author anonymized]".
    • Clean Word file metadata (File > Info > Inspect Document).
1.2 Length and Heading Hierarchy
  • Length: Between 1,000 and 10,000 words (including references). Significant excesses will require justification or will be returned for reduction.
  • Hierarchy (Headings): Strictly use the default styles of your word processor (Heading 1, Heading 2, Heading 3). Do not simulate hierarchies using only bold or manual font size increases.
Required structure:
Heading 1: Results
  Heading 2: Descriptive Analysis of the Survey
    Heading 3: Variations by Age Group
1.3 Citation and References (Chicago 17 Author-Date)

The journal uses the Chicago Manual of Style (17th ed.), Author-Date system. It requires parenthetical citation in the text and an alphabetical list at the end.

  • In-text citation:
    Paraphrase: The evolution of species is a gradual process (Darwin 1859).
    Direct quote: (Darwin 1859, 78).
  • Reference List (Scientific Journal Example):
    Dittmar, Emily L., and Douglas W. Schemske. 2023. "Temporal Variation in Selection Influences Microgeographic Local Adaptation." American Naturalist 202 (4): 471-85. https://doi.org/10.1086/725865.
  • The use of bibliographic managers (Zotero, Mendeley) is strongly recommended. Every article cited in the text must appear in the references, and vice versa.
1.4 Classification: Keywords and Codes

To ensure global indexing, you must provide exact identifiers:

  • Keywords: 3 to 6 terms (in English and Spanish).
  • Controlled Vocabularies: You must search for the exact alphanumeric code corresponding to your discipline. Only enter the code in the system, not the text.
    • Health/Medicine: MeSH thesaurus (e.g., D008112)
    • Economics: JEL classification (e.g., F50)
    • Computer Science: ACM CCS classification
    • Humanities: UNESCO thesaurus
    • Mathematics: MSC 2020 classification
1.5 Ethics, Originality, and Artificial Intelligence
  • Originality: Maximum tolerance for similarity (plagiarism) of 15% detected by specialized software (e.g., Turnitin/iThenticate).
  • Use of AI: The use of AI (LLMs) is permitted exclusively for improving writing or translation. It is strictly prohibited to use it to generate data, analyses, scientific images, or conclusions. Its use must be declared in the cover letter.
  • Data Availability: It is mandatory to include a "Data Availability Statement" at the end of the manuscript, indicating in which public repository the data is located, or the justification for its privacy.
  • Conflicts of Interest: Mandatory declaration of funding or relationships that may bias the research.

3. Editorial Review Flow

Transparency is a pillar of our journal. This is the standard journey of your manuscript from submission to publication.

1

Technical Screening (Desk Review)

The management team verifies anonymity, format, similarity (plagiarism), and thematic focus. If it does not meet the basic requirements, it is returned (Desk Reject).

2

Assignment and Peer Review

A Section Editor assigns the manuscript to 2 or 3 external expert reviewers (double-blind). They evaluate methodological rigor, originality, and contribution to knowledge.

3

Editorial Decision

Based on the reports, the Editor issues a decision:

  • Accept: Goes directly to production.
  • Minor / Major Revisions: You must correct the manuscript and submit a detailed response letter to the reviewers.
  • Reject: Insurmountable methodological weaknesses are notified.
4

Production and Open Publication

The accepted manuscript is typeset, assigned a DOI (Digital Object Identifier), and published under a Creative Commons license (Diamond Open Access, with no APC charges).
