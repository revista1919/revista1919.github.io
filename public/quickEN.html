Quick Guide for Authors

If this is your first time publishing an article, you are in the right place. Here we explain, step by step and with examples, everything you need to have ready before submitting your manuscript.

Essential notice: This guide is a didactic summary. It must not replace reading the official policies, but it will help you understand the basic requirements and avoid common mistakes. It is at your discretion to review the Complete Guide for more specific details.

1. Understanding the key requirements

1.1 The file and anonymity
  • File format: You must submit your work in Microsoft Word (.docx). If you use LaTeX, convert the file with tools like Pandoc and check that everything looks correct.
  • What is double-blind review? It means that neither the reviewers know who you are, nor do you know who they are. For this to work, your document must not show your name, your school or university, or any clues about your identity.
  • How do I do it?
    • Do not write your name on the cover page or in the header.
    • Check that in "acknowledgments" or "project information" there are no proper names or institutions that identify you.
    • If you cite your own previous works, replace them with "[Anonymized for review]". Don't worry: if your article is accepted, you will restore it.
  • IMRaD structure: It is the recommended skeleton for research articles: Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion. It helps you organize your ideas clearly.
Visual example: If your article is called "The effect of music on concentration," the "Methods" section will explain how many participants you had, what type of music you used, and how you measured concentration. That is the logic.
1.2 How long should it be? How do I structure the text?
  • Recommended length: Between 1,000 and 10,000 words counting references. If your work is a little longer, that's fine, but if it goes too far, the editor may suggest cutting parts that are not essential.
  • Headings: Do not make titles with a larger font and manual bold. Use the "Heading 1", "Heading 2" and "Heading 3" styles that come in Word or Google Docs.
    This is what it looks like:
    Heading 1: Results
      Heading 2: Survey analysis
        Heading 3: Comparison between groups
    This makes your document navigable and professional. It is a mandatory requirement.
1.3 How to cite correctly (Chicago 17)
  • Official style: The journal uses Chicago 17th edition, Author-Date mode. This means that in the text you put the author's last name and the year, and at the end of the document you put the complete list of references.
  • In the text:
    "The evolution of species is a gradual process (Darwin 1859)"
    If you quote directly, add the page: (Darwin 1859, 78)
  • Reference list (journal article example):
    Dittmar, Emily L., and Douglas W. Schemske. 2023. "Temporal Variation in Selection Influences Microgeographic Local Adaptation." American Naturalist 202 (4): 471-85. https://doi.org/10.1086/725865.
  • Important: You must ensure that each citation in the text has its full reference at the end. If you use a manager like Zotero or Mendeley, great; if not, check carefully. See the Complete Guide for more examples (books, chapters, websites).
1.4 Keywords and specialized codes (controlled vocabularies)
  • What are keywords? They are 2 to 6 words or short phrases that describe your topic. You write them in Spanish and then their equivalents in English. Example: "marxism, political economy, surplus value".
  • What are specialized codes? They are numeric or alphanumeric identifiers used by databases to classify your article. You only need to write the code, not the full name.
  • How do I find my code? It depends on your area. Here are the most common direct links:
    • Biology / Medicine / Health: MeSH (Medical Subject Headings)
    • Economics / Sociology / Politics: JEL Classification System
    • Computer Science / AI / Robotics: ACM CCS (Computing Classification System)
    • History / Philosophy / Linguistics: UNESCO Thesaurus
    • Psychology: APA Thesaurus (PsycINFO)
    • Agronomy / Veterinary: AGROVOC (FAO Thesaurus)
    • Can't find your area? Check the complete table in Editorial Policies (Section 3.5.1).
  • Concrete example:
    If your article is about economics, go to JEL and search for "Marxism". You will find the code B14. In the submission form, you only write B14. Do not write B14: Marxism.
1.5 Ethics, originality, and use of AI
  • Similarity (plagiarism): Your work must be original. If you use fragments from others (even if you cite them), the similarity percentage should not exceed 15%. This is checked with specialized software, so write in your own words and cite correctly.
  • Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI): You can use ChatGPT or similar to correct spelling or improve writing, but you must declare it in the form. You cannot use AI to generate the research, results, or scientific images.
  • Open data: You must include a Data Availability Statement. It is a short phrase explaining where your data is or why it cannot be shared.
    Example: "The data supporting this study are available in the institutional repository of ... University (link)."
    If not applicable: "No new datasets were generated for this article."
  • Funding and conflicts: If you received money or support for your research, declare it. If not, write: "This research received no external funding."
